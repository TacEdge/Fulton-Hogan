What we would like to explore with Fulton Hogan

Under the Integrated Delivery Model a network is delivered by many parties: Fulton Hogan, NZTA, MSQA providers, directory suppliers. The systems you run are excellent at single-party answers. The strain is the shared, multi-party picture, on networks where roughly 40 percent of the country has no cellular coverage and the picture matters most exactly when weather takes the towers down.

We want to explore a thin coordination layer above the systems you already run, replacing none of them, satellite-enabled through One NZ so it keeps working when terrestrial networks do not. We are not here to pitch a product. We would rather understand how coordination actually strains on your networks, and test whether this is worth building with you.

The questions we are bringing
  1. 01Where does multi-party coordination strain hardest under IDM mobilisation? What does a bad day look like operationally?
  2. 02In the last significant storm response, how was the operational picture actually assembled?
  3. 03How does NZTA get visibility of network status and response progress during an event today?
  4. 04Where does connectivity bite hardest, and on which network would satellite change the answer?
The right next step

One network, validated together

If this is worth pursuing, the next step is small and real: pick one network and prove the shared picture is worth having. Integration with your systems comes only after the picture earns it.
